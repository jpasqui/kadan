An enemy in Pikmin 4 is a Blueprint with an animation Blueprint. Its class brings what it does:
its AI, the spheres Pikmin hit and latch onto, its life and how it is carried. Its animation
Blueprint brings how it moves: the AI asks for states such as `Wait`, `Attack` and `Die` by name,
and waits for each one to finish. You make a new enemy the same way the game makes its variants.
You take one of the game's enemy classes as the parent, give it your model, and give it a copy of
that enemy's animation Blueprint that plays your clips.

## Before you start

- [Import](/guides/import/) the game enemy whose behavior you want, for example
  `Placeables/Teki/GKochappy` for the Dwarf Bulborb. Kadan imports its Blueprints, its animation
  Blueprint (`Characters/Teki/Kochappy/ABP_Kochappy`) and its clips.
- Import your model and its clips from FBX, and make its materials. Any bone names work. If your
  model's scale or facing is off, use Kadan's rebake for skeletal assets.

## Steps

<Steps>

1. **Copy the animation Blueprint.** In the Content Browser, right-click the game enemy's
   animation Blueprint (`ABP_Kochappy`) and choose **Retarget Anim Blueprints > Duplicate Anim
   Blueprints and Retarget**. Pick your skeleton and a folder of your mod. Unreal makes a copy on
   your skeleton, with copies of the clips it plays.
2. **Put your clips in its states.** Open the copy. In its `Default` state machine, open each
   state and change the clip in its sequence player to yours. Keep every state's name: the AI
   asks for states by name. Keep one-shot states (`Die`, `Attack`, `Flick` and the like) from
   looping, because the AI waits for them to end. Notifies on the copied clips, such as `DieEnd`
   and the bite's `MouthStick` notifies, are what the AI listens for, so add the same notifies to
   your clips where your animation needs them.
3. **Make the enemy class.** Right-click the game enemy's base Blueprint
   (`Placeables/Teki/Base/GKochappyBase`) and choose **Create Child Blueprint Class**, in your
   mod's folder.
4. **Give it your body.** In the child, select the inherited `CarrotSkeletalMesh` component. Set
   **Skeletal Mesh** to your mesh, **Anim Class** to your animation Blueprint, and its materials
   to yours. Set the `Carcass` component's mesh to your corpse mesh if you have one.
5. **Add the AI.** If the base class has no AI component, add the one the game's enemy uses, for
   example `KochappyAI`. Compile and save.
6. **Declare it.** In your mod's folder, create **Miscellaneous > Treasure or creature**. Set
   **Kind** to Creature, a short name, **Actor Class** to your enemy class, **From** to the game
   enemy's row (for example `KOCHAPPY`), a display name, and its life.
7. **Export.** Add the declaration to your mod and [export](/guides/export/). Kadan ships the
   class, the animation Blueprint, your clips, model and materials.

</Steps>

## What happens in the game

Tsuchi registers your enemy as a new kind with its own identity, taking every value you left
unchanged from the game enemy's rows. It behaves like that enemy, using your model and your
animations: Pikmin attack it, the game's life gauge shows over it, it bites, and when it is
defeated it plays your `Die` state, turns into a carcass and Pikmin pick it up.
