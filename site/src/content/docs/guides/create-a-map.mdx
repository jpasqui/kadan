For this guide, we'll make a new daytime story area, starting with an empty level. If you want
to change one of the game's maps instead, follow [Edit a map](/guides/edit-a-map/).

Before you start, [install Kadan](/guides/install/) and fill in the
[export setup](/guides/install/#game-folder-and-project-setup). You'll also need the game classes and reference
assets that your map uses, so [import those](/guides/import/) into your project.

## Mod

Open **Kadan > Mods and export**, click **New mod**, and give your mod a **Name**, an **ID**, and a
**Version**, then click **Save**. For example, use `example.course` as the ID.

Make a folder for the mod in the Content Browser, for example `/Game/Mods/ExampleCourse`, and keep
the levels, scene, and assets that you create in it, so they stay together. Make a `Maps` folder
inside it for the map.

Give the area a short name, such as `course`. Its destination ID is your mod's ID and the short
name, joined by a colon. For example, `example.course:course` identifies this area.

## Levels

The game has four free area slots, numbered 5, 7, 8, and 9. A *build* is your map's files made for
one of those numbers. For the first build, choose an available slot. We'll use 5 in this example.

<Steps>

1. Choose **File > New Level**, then **Empty Level**. You should see an empty level in Unreal Editor.
2. Save it as `Area005_P` in your mod's `Maps` folder. This is the persistent level for this build.
3. Use Unreal's Levels window to add sublevels if you want to separate scenery, gameplay objects,
   or lighting. Save them in your mod's folder and use the same area number in their names.
4. Set the sublevels' streaming behavior for your map. Make sure the ground and first base are
   loaded when the player arrives, and save the persistent level with those sublevels attached.

</Steps>

For example, you can organize the map's assets like this:

```text
Maps/
  Area005_P
  SD_Area005
  Sublevels/
    Area005_Ground
    Area005_GameArea
```

The scene for this build will be named `SD_Area005`. The world, scene, sublevels, and placement
references must all agree on the area number. To make another build, author its files and
references for that number, such as `Area007_P` and `SD_Area007`.

You can include one to four builds of the same area. Tsuchi chooses an available build when the
mod starts. A map with all four builds can run beside three other added areas. Renaming a cooked
file doesn't change the area references inside it.

For more about creating levels and sublevels, see Epic's
[level creation guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/level-designer-quick-start?application_version=4.27)
and [Levels window guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/managing-multiple-levels?application_version=4.27).

## Setup

With the persistent level selected, configure its **World Settings** for the story map's
gameplay and lighting. Use an imported stock story map as a reference for the game's startup
actors and settings, then configure the required actors in your own level. Keep the gameplay
classes and other unchanged game assets as references to their game paths.

Place the map's first base on walkable ground and leave room for the captain, Oatchi, and the
Pikmin to arrive. The game starts the player at that base. If you add a **Player Start** for an
Unreal preview, the game's arrival still comes from the first base, rather than that preview
start point.

Create the map's `GameSceneData` and `ActorPlacedNumAsset` beside the saved level. For this build,
name the scene `SD_Area005` and set its `OpenLevel` reference to `Area005_P`. Set its required
scene references and counts for your map. Keep the counts asset consistent with your placements,
as the game uses it for totals and completion.

## Ground

Add your ground and scenery by dragging meshes from the Content Browser into the level. You can
use unchanged game meshes as references, or import meshes of your own into your mod's folder.
Keep your own textures and materials there too. Check
[Assets and replacements](/guides/export/#assets) for the source-content rules.

Give the ground and obstacles the collision they need. Check that the player can stand on the
ground and that ramps, doors, and routes have enough space for Pikmin. Cover the playable ground
with navigation bounds and use the map's navigation-agent settings when you build paths.

## Placements

Drag the Blueprints for enemies, treasures, and gates into the level, then set their placement
options in the **Details** panel. Kadan exports gameplay objects as native placement lists, while
scenery stays in your map's levels. Use a content ID and dependency when you place something
provided by another mod.

Keep the placement counts in your scene up to date as you add or remove objects. The game keeps
what happens to placed gameplay objects in the player's save, so defeated creatures and
collected treasures stay recorded after a reload. Put additions for an already-applied map in
a new placement list. See [Tables and placements](/guides/export/#tables-and-placements).

## Lighting and navigation

Place the lights your map needs and configure its lighting in the persistent level's
**World Settings**. Rebuild lighting from Unreal's **Build** menu, then choose
**Build > Build Paths** to rebuild navigation.

Inspect the lighting and walkable routes, then save every level and its build data. Kadan exports
the lighting build data and navigation with the map. After changing ground, collision, or
lighting, rebuild the affected data before you export again.

## Area

Set the area's display name and include each world/scene pair as a build. You can also give the
area a preview texture and a radar texture. The radar's size is the width of the pictured world
in centimeters, and its offset and rotation align the picture with your level.

Choose the Pikmin icons that the area list should show and set any declared treasure, castaway,
and Flarlic totals. The supported area-list icons are red, blue, yellow, rock, winged, purple,
white, and ice Pikmin. The declared totals help the area list display your area, while the
scene's counts and the save determine placements and collected progress.

Kadan writes the area under `new_maps` in your mod's `mod.json`. For example, a minimal declaration
for one build is:

```json
{
  "id": "example.course",
  "name": "Terrain Course",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "new_maps": {
    "course": {
      "name": "Terrain Course",
      "builds": [{
        "world": "/Game/Mods/example_course/Maps/Area005_P",
        "scene": "/Game/Mods/example_course/Maps/SD_Area005.SD_Area005"
      }]
    }
  }
}
```

Use the paths from your own mod's content folder in place of `example_course`. `world` takes a
package path, while `scene` takes an object path with the asset name after the dot. The game's
first base and startup configuration belong in the level and scene, and the manifest names those
built assets.

## Export

<Steps>

1. Open **Kadan > Mods and export** and choose your mod.
2. Add the map, scene, or their folder from the Content Browser to your mod's selection. Kadan
   follows those roots and includes the assets they require.
3. Read What ships. If the independent-world route is refused,
   stop there. Changing IDs or renaming cooked files does not complete its missing lifecycle.
   Keep the refusal when reporting the authoring gap.
4. Click **Export mod**. Your output should contain the mod's `romfs` folder, its manifest and
   supporting files, and the packed content for the new area.

</Steps>

See [Export a mod](/guides/export/) for What ships and where mods go.

## Testing

When an independent-world build is admitted, use the following checks.

[Install Tsuchi](https://tsuchi.jpasqui.dev/players/install-tsuchi/) and
[install your exported mod](https://tsuchi.jpasqui.dev/players/install-a-mod/), then start the
game. The new area should appear in the story's area list with the name you gave it. Travel there
and check that the player arrives at the first base.

Walk around the map, send Pikmin over ramps and around obstacles, and check the lighting, radar,
and placements. Save and reload, then leave and return to make sure the map and its progress
load again. Try the mod beside another new-area mod to check its build assignments.

If the area doesn't appear or load, check the mod list's reason and
[Tsuchi's log](https://tsuchi.jpasqui.dev/develop/logs/). A missing world or scene, mismatched area
names, or occupied build slots should give a reason. For editor problems, check
[Troubleshooting](/guides/troubleshooting/) and [Report a problem](/guides/get-help/).

## Progress

Once Tsuchi reserves an area number for the player's story, that number stays with the map,
including when its mod is removed. Preparing the area list can reserve it before the first
visit. Keep a build for that assigned number in later releases, so reinstalling the mod restores
the player's progress. The assignment covers the player's save slots.

End the day before removing the map mod. If a save made inside your area loads without the mod,
the game returns to the title screen and leaves the save untouched. Reinstalling the mod lets
the player load the area again. A save made at the base after ending the day loads without it.
